# QSR Market Intelligence Brief — Philippines Pizza & Pasta Landscape 2026
## Greenwich vs. Domino's: Strategic Outmaneuver Playbook

**Executive summary (3 sentences):**  
The Philippines QSR pizza-pasta market is pivoting from delivery-first to hybrid dine-in/delivery as aggregator commissions (20–33%) and consumer fatigue reshape unit economics. Greenwich can outmaneuver Domino's by owning **local occasions** (student groups, afternoon merienda, family weekends) through bundled value, time-bound flash sales, and experiential in-store kids' menus—without cutting core prices. The next 12 months will see a **15–20% share shift** back to physical group dining among Gen Z and families, rewarding chains that balance app acquisition with brick-and-mortar community hubs.

---

## Section 1 — Top 3 trends reshaping PH QSR pizza-pasta (2025–2026)

### Trend 1: Aggregator commission creep + profitability reset

**1) Insight — what is happening:**  
GrabFood and Foodpanda commissions have settled at **20–33% effective rates** (standard 22–30% + 2–8% marketing fees), forcing QSRs to either raise app prices, accept margin erosion, or drive direct ordering. [203][204][206][209][212]

**2) Evidence — local market reality / operational metric:**  
- On a ₱150 order, merchants net **₱105–₱112.50** after 25–30% commission + platform fees, before ingredients or rent. [204]  
- Cloud kitchens and delivery-heavy concepts report **15–30% of sales lost to commissions**, turning modest ₱300K/month delivery into ₱45K–₱90K profit black holes. [206][212]  
- Premium tiers (e.g., Foodpanda pandaPro) can cut commissions to **12–15%** but require volume commitments and exclusivity. [205][209]

**3) So what — implication for Greenwich strategy:**  
- **Tier 2 flash sale (₱56 mini)** must be **app-only but direct** (Greenwich app/website) to avoid 25–30% aggregator bleed.  
- Use aggregators for **discovery** (featured listings) but **retarget** to direct channels with loyalty perks (e.g., "Order direct, get free delivery").  
- Price architecture must **bake in commission math**: e.g., ₱599 student bundle nets ₱364 CM direct vs. ~₱260 CM on aggregators at 28% take rate.

---

### Trend 2: Consumer fatigue with delivery apps → shift to group dine-in

**1) Insight — what is happening:**  
Post-pandemic, Filipino consumers—especially Gen Z and families—are **rebalancing from solo delivery to group dine-in** for social occasions, value perception, and experience. [214][224][225]

**2) Evidence — local market reality / operational metric:**  
- **53% of Gen Z restaurant discovery** now happens on TikTok; **67% trust UGC over branded content**; **54% visit after seeing TikTok**. [225][226]  
- Digital ordering platforms now emphasize **hybrid dine-in/delivery solutions** (QR menus, real-time pickup updates) as consumers seek flexibility. [214]  
- QSR growth is driven by **socially engaging dining experiences** (group meals, family bundles), not just convenience. [224]

**3) So what — implication for Greenwich strategy:**  
- **Tier 1 student combo** and **Tier 3 kids' experiential bundle** must be **in-store or pickup-first**, leveraging group dynamics to lift AOV and margin.  
- Invest in **in-store experience** (staff-led games, customizable toppings) to differentiate from Domino's delivery-only model.  
- Use social proof (TikTok reviews, parent group shares) to **drive physical traffic** without discounting core menu items.

---

### Trend 3: Gen Z discovery via social media → decline of search-based ordering

**1) Insight — what is happening:**  
Gen Z's path to purchase has shifted from **Google/search engines to TikTok/Instagram discovery**, with **70% identifying TikTok as most valuable for food recommendations**. [220][221][223][226]

**2) Evidence — local market reality / operational metric:**  
- **TikTok functions as a discovery engine** for Filipino Gen Z in Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao—algorithm-driven, not search-based. [223]  
- **84% of Gen Z try social media food trends**; **71% influenced by social media**; **82% research social before new restaurants**. [225][226]  
- Short-form video (Reels/TikTok) dominates discovery; **feed posts drive consideration**; **UGC most trusted**. [225]

**3) So what — implication for Greenwich strategy:**  
- **Nano-influencer collabs** (0–10k followers) for Tier 1 and Tier 2, geo-fenced to campuses and office districts.  
- **TikTok-first creative** (15–20 sec hooks, "sulit" messaging) for student combo and flash sale; **Instagram carousels** for family bundles.  
- Track **social-driven foot traffic** (QR codes in bios, UTM links) to measure ROI beyond app downloads.

## Section 2 — Two emerging threats (microeconomic risks under-tracked)

### Threat 1: Ingredient inflation (cheese, wheat, dairy) + peso depreciation

**1) Insight — what is happening:**  
Philippine restaurants face **15–25% YoY ingredient cost inflation**, with imported dairy, wheat, and cheese hit hardest by peso depreciation vs. USD. [217]

**2) Evidence — local market reality / operational metric:**  
- **Rice, cooking oil, imported dairy/wheat** up 15–25% YoY; restaurants relying on imports (pizza cheese, pasta wheat) are **especially vulnerable**. [217]  
- Food inflation persists despite government intervention; **consumer purchasing power erodes**, forcing trade-down to cheaper options. [217]

**3) So what — implication for Greenwich strategy:**  
- **Lock in supplier contracts** for cheese/wheat 6–12 months ahead; explore **local dairy substitutes** (e.g., blended cheese) for Tier 2 mini pizza.  
- **Menu engineering**: shift mix toward **rice-based pizzas** (Greenwich's Pizza Rice Bake) and **local toppings** (longganisa, adobo) to hedge import exposure.  
- **Price architecture**: embed **inflation buffers** in Tier 1 and Tier 3 bundles (higher AOV, lower price sensitivity).

---

### Threat 2: Gen Z student groups → value-seeking + social validation

**1) Insight — what is happening:**  
Gen Z students (baon ₱100–₱150/day) prioritize **value-for-money ("sulit")** and **social validation** (TikTok-worthy meals) over brand loyalty. [171][172][220]

**2) Evidence — local market reality / operational metric:**  
- **40% of allowance spent on food/drinks**; daily baon ₱100–₱150 for lunch + merienda. [171][172]  
- **75% order based on influencer recommendations**; **56% social media buyer rate** (vs. 36.5% total population). [200][220]  
- **Discovery-driven consumption**: TikTok/Instagram drive unplanned food purchases; UGC > branded content. [222][225]

**3) So what — implication for Greenwich strategy:**  
- **Tier 1 student combo (₱599 for 3–4 pax)** must be **baon-friendly** (₱149–₱199 per head) and **TikTok-shareable** (overload visuals, group selfies).  
- **Peer-to-peer amplification**: incentivize barkada posts (e.g., "Tag 3 friends, get free drink") to leverage social proof.  
- **Dynamic pricing**: offer **flash discounts** (5–6%) for student groups during 2–5 PM, but **bundle-only** to protect core margins.

## Section 3 — Hot take prediction (12-month forecast)

**Bold forecast:**  
By Q3 2027, **15–20% of PH QSR pizza-pasta delivery volume** will shift back to **physical group dining** (dine-in + pickup), driven by Gen Z social occasions and family weekends. Chains that invest in **community-centric experiences** (e.g., Greenwich's Tier 3 kids' experiential bundle) will gain **5–7% share** from delivery-only players (e.g., Domino's cloud kitchens). [224][225][227]

**Rationale:**  
- **Aggregator fatigue** (20–33% commissions) + **consumer desire for social dining** will push families/students to **in-store value bundles**.  
- **TikTok-driven discovery** favors **visual, experiential dining** (customizable pizzas, staff-led games) over static delivery menus.  
- **Greenwich's local brand equity** (240+ stores, pizza-pasta leader) positions it to **own physical occasions** while Domino's remains delivery-centric. [219][227]

## Section 4 — Three strategic recommendations (tailored to your 3 proposals)

### Rec 1: Student Combo (Tier 1) — "Barkada Bundle" engine

**Action:**  
Launch **₱599 Overload Bundle (2 pizzas + pasta + 4 drinks)** for 3–4 pax, valid 2–5 PM weekdays, **app-only direct ordering** (no aggregators).

**Why:**  
- Defends student share vs. Domino's app discounts; lifts AOV to **₱149–₱199 per head** (baon-friendly). [171][172]  
- **61% margin** (₱364 CM) protects against inflation; **TikTok-shareable** visuals drive peer amplification. [225][226]

**KPI:**  
- **Bundle mix %** (target 15% of student orders), **new app sign-ups** (target 5K/month), **repeat rate** (30-day, target 25%).

---

### Rec 2: Merienda Flash Sale (Tier 2) — "₱56 Mini" profitable loss leader

**Action:**  
Run **₱56 mini pizza (1 topping)** flash sale, 4–6 PM Wed & Fri, **direct app-only** with **55% beverage + 25% side attachment** targets.

**Why:**  
- Wins afternoon slump (corporate/student merienda) without cannibalizing core menu; **₱56.7 CM per ticket** with attachments.  
- **Van Westendorp OPP** (₱56) anchors bargain perception; **explicit end time** ("Ends 6 PM") drives urgency. [196][197]

**KPI:**  
- **Redemption rate** (target 8–12% of afternoon orders), **attachment %** (55% drink, 25% side), **net incremental CM** (target ₱35+ per flash ticket).

---

### Rec 3: Experiential Kids Menu (Tier 3) — "Ear-Pizza + Game" family magnet

**Action:**  
Introduce **₱199 Kids Ear-Pizza Bundle** (mini pizza + side + drink + dessert + staff-led game), weekends 11 AM–8 PM, **in-store only**.

**Why:**  
- Owns family weekend occasions; **86% margin** (₱171.8 CM) offsets Tier 2 loss leader economics.  
- **Differentiates from Domino's** (no in-store experience); drives **physical traffic** without discounting core menu. [224][225]

**KPI:**  
- **Kids bundle attachment** (target 20% of family orders), **family ticket lift** (target +₱400 vs. baseline), **repeat rate** (30-day, target 35%).
